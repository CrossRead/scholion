Understand what is changing in your health.

Scholion connects laboratory history, recorded treatment, genetics and daily life. See the long-term direction, notice a recent reversal and bring a focused summary to your clinician.

0.7.0

From separate readings to a system-level picture.

A lower value is not always an improvement. Scholion checks the direction against recorded targets or laboratory ranges, keeps the year separate from the last quarter and month, and shows which observations support the finding.

For the patient: see the direction

The person map keeps body systems in view. Compact insight cards distinguish a long-term trend from emerging changes and show where the direction has recently reversed. Detailed readings remain one click away.

For the clinician: a prepared visit

Visit mode opens an organised sheet: findings by system, supporting charts, specific discussion questions and the recorded current regimen. Finished courses stay in history. Sources and reference genetics are available when needed.

Treatment, experience and measurements

Record a dated change in symptoms, function or tolerability. Review it alongside measurements before and during a recorded treatment course. A reported response and a measured change remain different kinds of evidence; neither alone proves treatment caused it.

Personal context behind a finding

Where a supported, read variant association matches the measured phenotype, Scholion adds the genetic evidence and its confidence level. Known lifestyle measurements provide context. A predisposition does not turn a normal laboratory result into an abnormal one. Clinicians can expand source links, call quality, applicability and observations in the other direction. Hypotheses remain explicitly labelled. Clinicians can compare several measurements with a diplotype and explore mechanisms across broad panels, including amino-acid, B12, lipid and phosphate metabolism. Reference hypotheses remain available when personal evidence is incomplete; they do not change the system score or establish a diagnosis.

One history, across every view

Recorded weight and wearable weight share a source-labelled timeline. BMI follows those same weights and the recorded height. Device monthly means remain distinguishable from individual readings, including in charts and assistant responses.

Useful with your assistant, too

The web app, CLI and MCP use the same findings. An assistant can retrieve the overview, build the visit agenda and compare with an explicitly saved review. Changed observations and revised interpretations remain distinguishable.

Two views for two moments

Personal mode helps you follow changes between visits. Visit mode brings the same facts into an agenda for the conversation with your clinician.

The local profile stays on your computer. If you share Scholion results or collected context with a hosted assistant, that information goes to its provider.

Bring what you already have

Start with a synthetic demo, then connect your own sources. A genome is optional: laboratory history and prescriptions are useful on their own. The held reference base links 1469 genes across fifteen body systems; this is a reference catalogue, not a count of diagnoses.

Laboratory history

Read PDF forms or enter a result by hand. Preserve the measurement date, unit and the reference printed on the form; a missing reference stays missing.

Genomic records

Read VCF and consumer array exports, including supported archive wrappers. BAM supports preparation and coverage workflows with external tools. A chip does not become a whole genome.

Prescriptions and wearables

Compare the current regimen with held pharmacogenomic support and measured phenotype. Track sleep, activity and recorded experiments without turning associations into treatment effects.

One core, several ways in

The local web app and CLI, a model skill, MCP and agent plugins use the same engine. The tool interface registers 40 tools. A model is optional: code computes the result; the assistant can phrase it.

Your records stay yours

No automatic PGS or whole-genome recalculation is needed for the new analytical views. Stored results remain available. If a newly curated ORAI1 or FOXO3 position is unread, genome updates identifies the missing position; re-genotyping is an explicit operation that needs suitable local inputs.

A single result within its recorded range can be reassuring about that measurement even when there is no trend. Missing trend points alone do not create a request for more tests.

What an answer can support

A research and educational tool, not a medical device, diagnosis or prescription. Clinical decisions belong to the patient and treating clinician.

Genotypes do not establish symptoms or treatment. Unread positions, incomplete coverage and missing support are explicit gaps.

Polygenic percentiles are model-dependent positions in a reference population, not probabilities of disease. Many source cohorts are predominantly European.

Core computations run locally. Optional reference lookups are explicit; SCHOLION_OFFLINE=1 blocks network requests. Connecting an assistant is a separate choice about what context it receives.

Open the demo in a minute

Python 3.10–3.13 on Linux, macOS or Windows. The PyPI package includes PDF reading; genome preparation uses separately installed bioinformatics tools.

pip install scholion
scholion init --demo
scholion overview
scholion serve

Use the demo first. For your own profile, follow the loading guide; these commands do not represent a clinical assessment.

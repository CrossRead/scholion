# Scholion — information for the clinician

Scholion brings a person's laboratory history, recorded prescriptions, genome and wearable measurements into one local profile. It supports preparation for an appointment; it does not diagnose or prescribe treatment.

## Start with the visit sheet

Choose **Visit** in the application header. The first tab opens a structured sheet with topics grouped by body system, the conclusions and supporting measurements, questions for discussion and current prescriptions. Completed courses remain in history. Sources and detailed observations can be expanded; the sheet can be printed.

## Read change at the right timescale

**Year** describes a long-term direction; **quarter and month** describe emerging changes when the measurements are comparable. A recent decline can coexist with a rise over a longer interval. Dates, units and data sources accompany the observations. Weight and BMI combine recorded and Garmin measurements in one timeline, with the source and aggregation period retained for each point.

Reported changes in symptoms, function or tolerability are shown separately from measured change. An improvement after the recorded start of a prescription establishes timing, not proof that the treatment caused it. Genetics and known lifestyle observations provide context, not a substitute for examination.

## How to read genetics

System panels show the actual reading, gene function, variant context and the **Scholion A–E evidence level**. Open the letter for its definition and the gene for details and sources. A gene–disease relationship from **GenCC** carries its own validity classification; it is distinct from evidence for an individual variant. A missing call does not mean the gene or allele is absent.

A **PGS percentile is a position in a reference population, not the probability of disease**. Calculation, coverage, coordinates and calibration must be verified. Previously saved unverified values retain their status and an explanation. Clinical findings require their own supporting evidence; a catalogue association alone does not establish a diagnosis.

## Use the observations, keep the decision clinical

A single recent measurement within its recorded range may be sufficient to describe the current finding. Missing trend points alone are not a reason for more tests. Additional testing needs a specific indication or an existing monitoring plan. Targets, treatment decisions and follow-up belong to the treating clinician.

The same computed facts are available in the local application, CLI and MCP interface. A language assistant receives those facts and their provenance; its wording still needs clinical review. The profile remains local unless the person chooses an external assistant or another explicit data transfer.
